# Employee-Turnover-Prediction-at-Salifort-Motors

📌 Project Overview

Salifort Motors is experiencing a high rate of employee turnover, leading to increased hiring costs, lost productivity, and organizational disruption.
This project uses data analytics and machine learning to predict employee turnover and identify the key factors driving employee departures.

The goal is to provide actionable, ethical insights that leadership and HR teams can use to improve employee retention and job satisfaction.

🎯 Business Objective

Predict whether an employee is likely to leave the company

Identify the most influential factors contributing to turnover

Translate analytical findings into clear business recommendations

🧠 Modeling Approach

Two models were built and evaluated:

Logistic Regression (Primary Model)

Chosen for interpretability and transparency

Allows leadership to understand why employees leave

Random Forest Classifier (Secondary Model)

Used to validate results and compare performance

Provides feature importance rankings

Evaluation Metrics

Recall (to identify at-risk employees)

ROC–AUC (overall model discrimination)

📂 Dataset

14,999 employee records

Key features include:

Satisfaction level

Number of projects

Average monthly hours

Time at company

Promotion history

Department

Salary level

Target variable:

left (1 = employee left, 0 = employee stayed)

🔍 Key Insights

Low satisfaction level is the strongest predictor of turnover

Employees with excessive monthly working hours are more likely to leave

Lack of promotion opportunities significantly increases attrition risk

A small number of features explain most turnover behavior

💡 Business Recommendations

Proactively monitor employee satisfaction

Balance workloads to reduce burnout

Increase transparency and frequency of promotion opportunities

Use model outputs to support retention efforts, not punitive decisions

⚖️ Ethical Considerations

Models are designed to support employees, not penalize them

Predictions indicate risk, not certainty

Transparency and interpretability were prioritized over complexity
